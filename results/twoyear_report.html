Correcting an earlier conclusion

An earlier version of this page reported that two years of data showed no edge, on a t‑statistic of 0.53. That measurement was taken on a configuration carrying a stop-loss the live strategy does not have. It is withdrawn.

What the stop does is not subtle. Over Dhan’s own window it converts a 64% win rate into 49%, and ₹102,022 into ₹34,615 — because the spreads it closes are the ones that would have recovered by the 15:15 exit. The strategy is paid to sit through exactly the moves the stop refuses to sit through.

A gap to Dhan remains — ₹102,022 against ₹156,712 — and it is honestly a signal-reconstruction gap, not a costing one. The replica picks the same direction as Dhan on 70% of shared trading days. The other 30% is what is still missing.

Removing the stop is the whole correction. It is the only change that moves the result materially, and it moves it by ₹67,407 per lot over thirteen months. Win rate goes from 48.8% to 64.1%, and the t‑statistic from 0.57 to 1.66.

A hypothesis that did not survive: Dhan enters 67% of its trades within fifteen minutes of 10:15, which looked like a tight entry window. Restricting the replica to 10:15–10:30 made things worse — ₹30,691 against ₹102,022 — because the remaining third of Dhan’s entries, spread across the rest of the window, are not the marginal ones. The wide window in the spec is correct; the entries are simply front-loaded.

Where the money actually goes

Half the gross profit is consumed by costs.

A four-leg round trip — two legs in, two legs out — on a spread that collects roughly 100 to 140 points of credit means the fee load is structural, not incidental. Every trade pays about ₹111 in charges against an average net of ₹105.

Every stopped trade is a loss by construction, so the 0% is not informative on its own. What is informative: the two columns nearly cancel. The strategy is ₹708k of held winners minus ₹665k of stops, and the ₹43k that survives is the small difference between two large opposing numbers — which is precisely why small changes to the stop swing the result so violently.

How far to trust this

What was verified, and what was not.

Two checks were run before any of the above was written down.

verified  The P&L engine is exact. Against the project's own saved backtest, every trade that both runs took on the same timestamp produced an identical net P&L — 188 of 188 matches, maximum difference ₹0.0000. Fills, charges and slippage are not in question.

partial  Signal selection is approximate. Against the live paper trader over a two-week overlap, this backtest reproduced 6 of 11 trades on the same date, direction and strike, with net P&L within about ₹200 per trade. It missed four the live engine took and added one it did not. Borderline signals near the 0.8 / 0.2 alpha thresholds resolve differently between a live feed and a reconstruction, and the live engine will sometimes hold more than one position where this backtest holds one.

That gap matters for predicting any individual trade and matters much less for the conclusions here, which rest on aggregates over 406 trades: the cost ratio, the drawdown-to-profit ratio, the year split, and the instability of the stop.

One number in the project README does not reproduce: it reports +₹28k for Jan–Jul 2026, and this run gives +₹5,958 over the same months. The saved trade file matches the README, so the difference is in which trades were selected, not in how they were priced — consistent with the alpha window parameters having been re-tuned on 20 Jul 2026, after that file was generated.

What to do about it

One change is clearly indicated; the rest is open.

1. Take the stop out of the live paper desk. It is currently running SL 15% of margin — a rule the strategy being replicated does not have, and which cost ₹67,407 per lot across Dhan’s window. Seven of the desk’s first twenty-two trades exited on it.

2. Accept that risk then needs another home. A credit spread is defined-risk — the wing caps the loss at (width − credit) × lot — so removing the stop does not create unlimited exposure. It does mean the worst case per trade is the full spread width, and position size becomes the only control. Dhan varies between 2 and 6 lots against roughly ₹18,000 of margin per lot.

3. Close the remaining signal gap before trusting any further tuning. The replica agrees with Dhan on direction on 70% of shared trading days. Until that number is higher, a backtest of this strategy cannot settle questions finer than the one settled here — and no parameter should be tuned on it.
